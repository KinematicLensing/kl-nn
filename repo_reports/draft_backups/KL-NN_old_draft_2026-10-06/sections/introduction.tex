\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Weak lensing (WL) is a powerful probe of dark matter and the growth of large-scale structure. In the coming decade, its science cases, including cosmic shear, galaxy-galaxy lensing, and cluster lensing, will reach unprecedented precision with data from Stage-IV surveys. Imaging surveys such as LSST \citep{DESC_SRD}, Roman \citep{Roman_AFTA,HLIS-Multiprobe,HLIS-LSST}, and Euclid \citep{Euclid} will measure the shapes of galaxies over large areas of the sky, while spectroscopic surveys such as DESI \citep{DESI1,DESI2} and SPHEREx \citep{spherex} will provide spectra for tens of millions of galaxies. Much work is being done in data analysis to make the most of this data. This work represents such an effort as we continue the development of kinematic lensing (KL), a shear measurement technique that reduces shape noise in WL measurements by up to an order of magnitude. Specifically, we explore ways to accelerate KL inference using neural networks.

While weak lensing has been able to achieve good constraints on cosmological parameters \citep{DES_Y3_3x2pt, DESY6CS}, there are many aspects of statistical uncertainty involved with its measurement. Among these is the so-called shape noise, which is caused by the degeneracy between the intrinsic shape of a galaxy with its shear effect. The intrinsic shape of a galaxy cannot be measured purely by photometry, giving rise to this source of uncertainty. In WL studies, shape noise is compensated for by averaging over a large number of galaxy samples, but methods to directly reduce shape noise are very limited.

KL was developed with the primary goal of reducing shape noise in individual galaxy measurements \citep{KL}. Using spectroscopic measurements of disk galaxies, KL is able to measure their velocity field, which, combined with the Tully-Fisher relation (TFR), constrains their intrinsic ellipticity and thereby reduces shape noise. Pranjal R. S. et al.~\citep{KLInferenceI} showed with simulated analyses that KL reduces shape noise from $\sigma_\epsilon^\mathrm{WL}\approx0.26$ to $\sigma_\epsilon^\mathrm{KL}\approx0.022$--$0.041$, and later made the first detection of a cluster lensing signal with KL, with a typical shear uncertainty of $\sigma(g_+)\approx0.026$, an order of magnitude improvement over WL shape noise \citep{KLCluster}. Xu et al.~\citep{KLRoman} forecasted that a KL analysis with Roman would improve the figure of merit over a traditional WL analysis by a factor of 1.70 in $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$--$S_8$ and 3.65 in $w_0$--$w_a$. Another forecast for DESI showed that a 14,000 deg$^2$ DESI-KL survey could place highly interesting constraints on structure formation at very low redshift, $\sigma_8(z<0.15)$, which would provide unique insights into the $S_8$ tension if it consolidates \XW{is it still a thing?}, while complementing LSST with a different sky footprint \citep{KLDESI}.

Machine learning methods have also been applied to traditional WL shear measurement. \textsc{Forklens} \citep{Forklens} measures galaxy shapes with a convolutional neural network (CNN) followed by a neural network calibration module. D$_4$CNN$\times$AnaCal \citep{D4CNN} combines a symmetry-enforcing CNN with analytical shear calibration \citep{Anacal}, and achieves multiplicative biases of $|m|<2\times10^{-3}$ for isolated galaxies. Neural posterior estimation (NPE) has also been used to infer shear directly from simulated multiband images \citep{NPEShear}. To this date, no neural network-based methods have been developed for KL.

In this work, we develop an NPE for KL inference. NPE is a type of simulation-based inference (SBI), which trains neural networks on data generated by a simulator to approximate the posterior \citep{SBIGuide}. Once trained, inference with NPE is amortized: the network can perform inference on new observations without additional training or simulations. Our model uses a CNN to extract features from a galaxy's photometric image and its DESI-like fiber spectra, and a conditional normalizing flow to estimate the joint posterior of shear and galaxy parameters. If successful, this method would greatly accelerate inference for the DESI-KL survey proposed in \citep{KLDESI}. We therefore build our simulations on the DESI and Legacy Survey instrument configurations used in that work, and test our model on simulated galaxies whose magnitudes, sizes, and SNRs follow the galaxy samples selected there.

We start off with a review of KL measurement basics in Sec.~\ref{sec:kl-basics}. In Sec.~\ref{sec:data-generate} we describe the simulation of our training, validation, and test sets. We describe our neural network architecture in Sec.~\ref{sec:nn-arch}, and validate the posteriors of our model in Sec.~\ref{sec:results}. In Sec.~\ref{sec:shear}, we explore how these posteriors can be turned into shear estimates. We discuss our results in Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}, and conclude in Sec.~\ref{sec:conclusion}.
