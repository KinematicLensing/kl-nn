Cluster Substructure Test

Two questions about our NPE shear likelihoods in a real cluster field. Does stacking them stay unbiased when the shear varies inside a cell? And can a κ map built from them show a subhalo with 30% of the host's mass, at the source density a DESI-like KL survey would have? Both are answered with 50 independent background-galaxy realizations of one cluster, analysed with the real NPE posteriors.

50 realizations · 350 jobs, none failed Cell stacking: no bias at the 3–4% level Likelihood map: subhalo at z > 2 in 60% of realizations Kaiser–Squires: z > 2 in 22%

Summary

  • Likelihood stacking stays unbiased when the shear varies inside a cell. Pooling all cells from 50 realizations, the stacked shear tracks each cell's reference shear with slope offsets m = +0.04 ± 0.04 (polar gt) and +0.02 ± 0.03 / +0.06 ± 0.03 (Cartesian g1/g2), and offsets c ≲ 3×10⁻⁴. A single cluster's cells span shears of only about 0.04, so this tests for bias at the 3–4% level. The percent-level m comes from the constant-shear stacks.
  • The error bars are honest, and slightly conservative. Pulls average 0 to within 0.04, with a spread of 0.81–0.86. Cells with one or two galaxies are pulled toward zero (−0.011 and −0.005 in gt). From three galaxies on, the precision-weighted residual is +0.0004 ± 0.0003.
  • The subhalo is visible in the likelihood κ map. Comparing each realization's map with the spread of no-subhalo maps, the subhalo stands out at a median z = 2.4 (16–84%: 0.9–3.4). It exceeds 2σ in 60% of realizations and 3σ in 28%.
  • Kaiser–Squires on the same galaxies does much worse: median z = 1.1, above 2σ in 22% of realizations. The gain comes from fitting the map to each galaxy's full likelihood instead of to cell averages.
  • The difference maps locate the subhalo. The difference map(main + sub) − map(main only) peaks within 0.5 Mpc of the subhalo in 38 of 50 realizations, with the peak about 7.6× the background rms. Averaged over the realizations, it is a clean blob on the subhalo.

Setup

The cluster is an NFW halo with M200c = 1015 M☉ (Duffy+08 concentration, c = 3.31) at zl = 0.13, where lensing is most efficient for sources at z = 0.3. The sub scenario adds an untruncated NFW subhalo with 30% of that mass (3×1014 M☉, c = 3.66) at (1, 0) Mpc. All sources are at z = 0.3. Shears come from GalSim's NFWHalo, summed in γ and κ before forming the reduced shear g = γ/(1 − κ).

The 50 realizations. Each uses its own random seed to draw:

  1. a galaxy count N ~ Poisson(n̄ A), with n̄ = 0.108 arcmin⁻² (Xu sample 3, the 75.5% behind the lens) and A = 1370 arcmin² (the 3 Mpc disk), so ⟨N⟩ = 148; the draws range from 118 to 179;
  2. positions uniform in the disk: r = R√u and φ uniform, with u uniform on [0, 1];
  3. galaxies: N rows, without replacement, from the Xu3 measured-axis test set (Rh ≥ 1″, vcirc from TF, catalog image and Hα S/N), each keeping its own size, inclination, orientation, velocity and S/N; only its shear is replaced by the cluster's.

Within a realization, the main and sub scenarios share their galaxies and their image and spectral noise seed, and differ only through the shear.

How the analysis works

Per-galaxy likelihoods

For each galaxy the NPE gives 104 TF-weighted posterior samples. Dividing by the training prior (uniform on |gk| < 0.1) leaves the shear likelihood Li(g). A kernel density estimate turns it into a smooth log-likelihood on a 401 × 401 grid over |gk| < 0.1 (spacing 5×10⁻⁴), read by bilinear interpolation. Each cell's stacked posterior is evaluated on a 201 × 201 grid. The grids are for speed: the κ fit evaluates every likelihood thousands of times. Doubling both grids changes the stacked cell shears by 10⁻⁶ on average and at most 4.5×10⁻⁵ in any cell, far below the 10⁻⁴ additive-bias level.

Cell stacking and the reference shear

Within a cell the galaxies' likelihoods are multiplied to give the posterior of one common shear G. Polar cells first rotate each likelihood into the tangential/cross frame at the galaxy's own position angle. The stacked shear is the posterior mean, and its error bar is the posterior sd.

The shear varies inside a cell, so the stack is compared with what it should converge to: the precision-matrix-weighted mean of the galaxies' true shears,

gref = (Σi Fi)−1 Σi Fi gi,true, Fi = inverse covariance of Li in the cell frame

This is Bernstein & Armstrong's (2014) quadratic posterior applied to a piecewise-constant shear model. Their per-galaxy curvature matrix Ai is our Fi. A pull is (stacked − reference)/σ. It averages 0 for an unbiased estimator and has spread 1 for honest error bars.

κ maps

Likelihood map (ours). Unknowns: κ on a 12 × 12 grid of 0.5 Mpc pixels. A trial map gives γ through the forward Kaiser–Squires kernel, then g = γ/(1 − κ) at each galaxy's exact position. The map is scored by Σi log Li(gi) plus a Gaussian prior that penalises large κ (σκ) and differences between neighbouring pixels. The most probable map is found with L-BFGS. This is a Wiener-filter-like reconstruction, with each galaxy's learned likelihood as the data term; its prior shrinks and smooths peaks on purpose to suppress noise.

Kaiser–Squires (standard). The stacked Cartesian-cell shears go on a zero-padded grid, the inverse kernel is applied in Fourier space, and the result is smoothed.

Difference maps and detection

The difference map is map(main + sub) − map(main only) for one realization, made with the same method and settings. The two datasets share their galaxies and noise, so the main halo, the shape noise and the prior's shrinkage cancel, and what remains shows how visible the subhalo is.

The single-map z is what one observed map could claim. Average the reconstructed κ over a 0.5 Mpc aperture on the subhalo position, and compare it with the same aperture in the 50 main-only maps: z = (asub − mean amain) / sd amain. No true κ is needed.

Details: repo_reports/KAPPA_FITTING_EXPLAINED.md (κ fitting, Wiener filter, uncertainties, resolution) and repo_reports/CELL_REFERENCE_SHEAR.md (reference shear, BA14 connection).

Choosing the κ-map settings (synthetic data only)

The settings were fixed before any NPE map was looked at. We used 20 synthetic noise realizations of one galaxy set, with Gaussian likelihoods matched to the NPE widths, and scored each setting by three numbers:

  • contrast: the subhalo signal divided by its noise. Signal = mean over realizations of the aperture value (reconstructed κ averaged over a 0.5 Mpc disk on the subhalo) in the main + sub maps, minus the same in the main-only maps. Noise = the scatter of the main-only aperture value across realizations;
  • false-peak rate: in the main-only maps, the fraction of (realization, null aperture) pairs whose value, relative to that aperture's own mean, exceeds the signal. There are 10 null apertures, on rings at 1 and 2 Mpc from the centre and at least 1.2 Mpc from the subhalo;
  • RMS error: the RMS of reconstructed minus true κ (main + sub) over the pixels inside the field.

The hyperparameters.

  • Pixel size: the side of the κ map's square pixels.
  • σκ: the prior's amplitude scale. The prior is −log p = [Σ κ² + ℓ² Σneighbours (κa − κb)²] / (2σκ²), so smaller σκ pulls the map harder toward zero.
  • Smoothness ℓ: in pixels; it weights the neighbour-difference term, so larger ℓ makes smoother maps.
  • KS smoothing: the σ of the Gaussian filter applied to the KS map, in cells (0.7 cells = 0.68 Mpc).

Rule, fixed in advance: the highest contrast with a false-peak rate ≤ 0.10. The grid was pixel {0.5, 0.75, 1} Mpc × σκ {0.03, 0.05, 0.1} × smoothness {0.5, 1, 2} px, and KS smoothing {0.5, 0.7, 1, 1.5} cells.

All slopes are within 2σ of zero; the largest is Cartesian g2 in the main scenario at 1.8σ. Main and sub share their noise, so their rows are not independent. Null test: without a subhalo the cross shear about the main halo should vanish. Its references are all ≈ 0, so no slope can be fitted; the precision-weighted mean stacked g× is −0.0003 ± 0.0003, with pulls −0.01 ± 0.02, so the null test passes. The ±0.03–0.04 precision is set by the narrow range of cell shears (mostly 0–0.04): at this level the test shows no extra bias from shear gradients inside cells.

Pull spread. If every cell's error bar σ were exactly right, the pulls would scatter with spread 1. A spread of 0.81–0.86 means the stacked shears scatter about 15% less than their error bars say: the errors are conservative. It is not a bias, and the detection significance is unaffected, because its null is measured empirically. The synthetic pipeline gives 0.88–0.92, so part of the effect is in the stacking itself, through the |G| < 0.1 bound and the KDE bandwidth. The remaining ~7% appears only with NPE likelihoods; its cause isn't established yet (pulls in the constant-shear stacks would tell).

Occupancy. One- and two-galaxy polar cells are pulled toward zero (−0.011 ± 0.003 and −0.005 ± 0.002). For cells with at least 3 galaxies, the unweighted mean gt residual is −0.0011 ± 0.0005, but weighted by each cell's precision it is +0.0004 ± 0.0003. So only the broad cells are shrunk; the precise cells, which dominate any likelihood-weighted result, are unbiased.

How z is computed. For each realization r:

  1. Aperture value a = the reconstructed κ averaged over a 0.5 Mpc disk centred on the subhalo position (200 random points, bilinear interpolation), for the main-only and the main + sub map.
  2. The null is the set of 50 values amain,r: what that spot looks like in maps of the same cluster without a subhalo, across noise and galaxy layouts (mean 0.0072, sd 0.0038 for the likelihood map).
  3. zr = (asub,r − meanr′ amain,r′) / sdr′ amain,r′.

So z asks how unusual realization r's subhalo spot would be for a map with no subhalo. It doesn't use r's own main-only map, which shares r's noise, so it's the significance one observed map could claim. Subtracting the true main-halo κ from every map would change nothing, since that is the same constant in every realization.

Assumption: the main halo is known

The null comes from simulated maps of the true main halo. For a real cluster it would come from simulations of the best-fit main-halo model, with the observed galaxy positions and noise. Uncertainty in that fit (mass, concentration, centre) would widen the null somewhat, so these z values are upper bounds in that sense. The halo-fit code is in place to quantify this: fit the main halo in each main + sub realization and build the null from the fitted model.

The typical significance is below the fixed-galaxy forecast of 3.0, for two reasons. The null now includes galaxy-layout variation as well as noise, widening it from 0.0031 to 0.0038. And the mean signal is 0.0084. How many galaxies happen to land near the subhalo matters: z correlates with the galaxy count (r = 0.32).

Synthetic validation of the stacking (30 realizations)

Before trusting the NPE run, the whole chain was run on synthetic Gaussian likelihoods with the NPE widths, for one galaxy set and 30 noise draws. Pulls average 0 to within 0.05, with a spread of 0.88–0.92. Two- and three-galaxy polar cells are pulled toward zero by 0.010 and 0.002. A few sparse cells sit 3–4σ off their reference when averaged over the draws, by up to 0.012. Recomputing the reference with exact precisions doesn't remove this, so it comes from the stack itself (the prior bound or the KDE's reflection at the box edge), at about 0.15 of a cell's error bar.

Corrections made while building this test

  1. Synthetic likelihood widths. The synthetic likelihoods were first about 16% too narrow, because a box-truncated posterior width was truncated again. They now match the NPE widths to within 6%. The tuning choice didn't change.
  2. S/N maps. The exact Hessian of the map objective is unusable with grid-interpolated likelihoods. It is replaced by the Gauss–Newton form, and S/N uses the map's noise covariance rather than its posterior covariance.
  3. Shared random draws. The synthetic main and sub likelihoods now share their Monte Carlo draws, as the NPE scenarios share their noise seeds. Without this, synthetic difference maps were noisy.
  4. Pixel-averaged true κ. The true κ is averaged over each pixel, because a point evaluation diverges on the KS cell centred on the halo.

What this means for the paper, and what's next

  • Claim 1 (Sec. 6.4). Likelihood stacking stays unbiased when the shear varies inside a cell: no extra bias at the 3–4% level across 50 cluster realizations, with honest (≈15% conservative) error bars. The percent-level m statement stays with the constant-shear test.
  • Claim 2 (Sec. 6.5). At sample-3 density, a 30% subhalo at 1 Mpc shows up above 2σ in about 60% of clusters with the likelihood map, against 22% for KS, and is located to within about 0.5 Mpc. Compared with KL-II's per-galaxy shear, the resolution is about 0.5–1 Mpc, because the NPE needs several galaxies per element.
  1. Sample-1 density (about 4× more galaxies) for a cleaner single-cluster detection figure, if wanted.
  2. Cause of the sparse-cell shrinkage: rerun the validation without the |G| < 0.1 bound and without KDE reflection.
  3. Extend the tuning grid past its edge (σκ = 0.02, 0.4 Mpc pixels).
  4. The NPE–MCMC comparison (Lauren's package) decides whether NPE likelihoods are as narrow as exact ones. The slightly conservative pulls here hint that they may be a little broad.
