Summary

  • The subhalo is plainly visible in the difference maps, map(main + sub) − map(main only), from the NPE posteriors. The likelihood map shows a compact blob centred on the subhalo, with a peak of 0.012 and a mean of 0.011 within 0.5 Mpc. Away from the subhalo the map is flat: rms 0.0016 beyond 1.5 Mpc. The peak is about 40% of the true subhalo κ in its pixel (0.030), because the smoothing prior spreads and lowers peaks.
  • Kaiser–Squires shows it too, but coarser: a peak of 0.008 spread over its 1 Mpc cells, so the subhalo is located only to within a cell.
  • The response matches the synthetic forecast: the mean difference in a 0.5 Mpc aperture on the subhalo is +0.0098 for NPE, against +0.0095 expected from synthetic data.
  • Cell stacking: no significant bias in the NPE run, with linear and cubic fits consistent with zero. Over 30 synthetic realizations the pulls average 0 with a spread of 0.88–0.92, so the error bars are about 10% conservative. Sparse cells (2–3 galaxies) are pulled toward zero by up to 0.01, and a few cells are biased at about 0.15 of their error bar. That doesn't matter for the maps.
  • Two fixes since the first report (details below): the synthetic likelihoods had been about 16% too narrow, and the first S/N maps used an invalid Hessian. Neither changes which settings were chosen.

Setup

The cluster and galaxies are as in round 1, except for the subhalo position. The main halo has M200c = 1015 M☉ (c = 3.31) at zl = 0.13. The subhalo is 3×1014 M☉ (c = 3.66), untruncated NFW. All sources are at z = 0.3. There are 141 galaxies within 3 Mpc (0.108 arcmin⁻², Xu sample 3 behind the lens). The main-scenario galaxies are identical to round 1; only the subhalo shear changed.

The largest reduced shear at a galaxy is 0.045. The subhalo changes the shear of 48 galaxies by more than 0.005, and of 12 by more than 0.01.

How the analysis works

Per-galaxy likelihoods and cell stacking

As in round 1: each galaxy's NPE shear posterior (104 TF-weighted samples) is divided by the training prior and turned into a smooth log-likelihood on a 401 × 401 grid in (g1, g2). Within a cell the likelihoods are multiplied, after rotating into the tangential/cross frame for polar cells. The stacked shear is the posterior mean, with the posterior sd as its error bar.

Each cell is compared with its reference shear. This is the precision-matrix-weighted mean of the galaxies' true shears, gref = (Σ Fi)−1 Σ Fi gi, which is what the stack converges to when the shear varies inside the cell. Bias fits regress stacked − reference on the reference: a linear fit (m, c) and a cubic fit (m, c, b3).

κ maps

Kaiser–Squires (KS): the stacked Cartesian-cell shears go on their grid, which is zero-padded. The inverse KS kernel is applied in Fourier space and the result is Gaussian-smoothed by 0.7 cell.

Likelihood map (ours): the unknowns are κ on a 12 × 12 grid of 0.5 Mpc pixels. A trial map gives γ through the forward KS kernel, then the reduced shear g = γ/(1 − κ) at each galaxy's exact position. The map is scored by Σi log Li(gi), using the same per-galaxy likelihoods, plus a Gaussian prior that penalises large κ (σκ) and pixel-to-pixel differences (smoothness length). The most probable map is found with L-BFGS.

Difference maps

The question here is how visible the subhalo is in a map with substructure compared with one without. So the residual is the difference between the two reconstructions, map(main + sub) − map(main only), made with the same method and settings. Both datasets have the same galaxies, the same parameters and the same noise draws, so whatever is common to them cancels: the main halo, the shape noise, and the prior's shrinkage of the main peak. What remains is the change the subhalo causes in the map. Each difference map is shown beside the true subhalo κ at the same resolution.

S/N maps

S/N = κ / σnoise per pixel. σnoise is how much the map's value scatters from one noise realization to the next:

Covnoise = H−1 Hd H−1, Hd = Σi JiT Fi Ji, H = Hd + Hprior

Ji is how galaxy i's predicted shear responds to each κ pixel, and Fi is the inverse covariance of its likelihood. This noise covariance predicts the measured scatter of the subhalo-aperture value across the 20 synthetic realizations to within about 12%, on the conservative side. The posterior width, H−1, would be about 2× larger here. That is because the strong prior adds its own allowance for what κ could be, which is the wrong yardstick for "is this blob noise?".

Tuning the κ maps on synthetic data

The settings were fixed before the NPE data were looked at. We ran 20 synthetic noise realizations of the same 141 galaxies. Each galaxy gets a Gaussian likelihood with its NPE shear width, and main and sub share the noise. Every setting on the grid was scored:

  • Contrast: the mean residual in a 0.5 Mpc aperture on the subhalo, sub data minus main data, divided by the scatter of that aperture across realizations of the main-only data. This is the expected detection significance.
  • False-peak rate: how often one of 16 null apertures, on rings at 1 and 2 Mpc away from the subhalo, fluctuates above the mean subhalo signal.
  • RMS error: the root-mean-square of the reconstruction minus the true κ over the field.

Rule, fixed in advance: take the highest contrast among settings with a false-peak rate ≤ 0.10. The grid was pixel {0.5, 0.75, 1.0} Mpc × σκ {0.03, 0.05, 0.1} × smoothness {0.5, 1, 2} pixels, and KS smoothing {0.5, 0.7, 1.0, 1.5} cells.

The choice sits at the edge of the grid

The best setting uses the smallest pixel and the smallest σκ tried, so the optimum may lie just beyond the grid. A smaller σκ shrinks both signal and noise. Contrast is still rising there, but noticeably (2.7 → 3.0 from σκ = 0.05 to 0.03 at 0.5 Mpc, smoothness 1). Extending to σκ = 0.02 and 0.4 Mpc pixels is cheap, and is listed under next steps.

The cell shears span only about ±0.02 (up to 0.04 in the central disk), so slope and offset are poorly separated in one realization: m is uncertain by 0.2–0.5, and b3 by hundreds. No slope reaches 2σ (the largest is −0.47 ± 0.25). The starred b3 is fitted to cross-shear references spanning only −0.012 to +0.006; it rests on the two extreme cells and is not meaningful. In the main scenario the g× references are all ≈ 0 by symmetry, so that slope is undefined and is left out of the table. The cubic fits, kept as asked, mostly show that a single realization can't constrain them.

One offset to watch: the Cartesian g1 offset is c = +0.004 ± 0.002 (main) and +0.006 ± 0.002 (sub), 1.9σ and 2.6σ. The two scenarios share their noise, so this is one fluctuation, not two. The synthetic run gives c ≈ 0.000 ± 0.002. The constant-shear tests limit the NPE's additive bias to the 10⁻⁴ level (10⁴c = 0.4 ± 1.4 at low |g|), so a real +0.005 is implausible. This is most likely noise, and the extra realizations will confirm it.

The χ² values are below their degrees of freedom and the pull spreads are 0.72–0.86, so the NPE stacked error bars are larger than the scatter in this realization. Main and sub share their noise, and polar and Cartesian share their galaxies, so these four numbers are close to one independent test. That is roughly a 1.5–2σ effect. The synthetic run has the same tendency (23.2/27, 27.0/35). Whether it persists needs more NPE noise realizations.

Stacking validation on synthetic data (30 realizations, s3_sub1)

We ran 30 synthetic noise realizations of the s3_sub1 galaxies, now with the corrected (realistic) likelihood widths. Each realization goes through the whole chain: KDE grids, cells, stacking, and the reference.

  • Pull means are 0 to within 0.05 (−0.04 to +0.01) in both scenarios and both layouts.
  • Pull spreads are 0.88–0.92, so the stacked error bars are about 10% conservative. That is the same direction as the NPE run (0.72–0.86 in its one realization), but smaller.
  • Sparse cells are pulled toward zero. Two-galaxy polar cells average −0.010 in gt, and three-galaxy cells −0.002. The one-galaxy Cartesian cells sit at +0.005. This is stronger than in round 1 (−0.005), because the corrected likelihoods are wider (about 0.07 before truncation, against the box's 0.1 half-width), so the |G| < 0.1 bound on the stack binds more often.
  • A few cells sit 3–4σ off their reference when averaged over the 30 realizations: two of 29 polar cells and one or two of 37 Cartesian cells. The largest offsets are about 0.012, in cells of 2–3 galaxies. Recomputing the reference with the exact (untruncated) synthetic precisions doesn't remove them, so they come from the stack itself, not from the choice of reference. The likely sources are the prior bound and the reflection at the box edges in the KDE, both of which matter for wide likelihoods. Across all cells with at least 3 galaxies, the cell biases average about 0.15 of a cell's own error bar (rms cell z = 1.2–1.3). That is negligible for the maps and the detection, but it means "unbiased" holds only to that level.

Fixes made during this round

  1. Synthetic likelihoods were too narrow. Each synthetic galaxy took its Xu3 source's posterior covariance as the width of a Gaussian, which was then cut to the training box. But that covariance comes from a posterior already inside the box, so broad galaxies were truncated twice. The synthetic likelihoods came out about 16% narrower than the NPE ones (median σg 0.037 against 0.049). Now each galaxy uses the Gaussian width whose box-truncated covariance matches its Xu3 posterior. The synthetic widths are within 6% of the NPE ones, which themselves match their Xu3 sources (median ratio 1.015). Rerunning the tuning with the corrected widths picks the same settings; the expected contrast drops from 3.4 to 3.0 (KS: 1.4 to 1.25).
  2. The S/N maps needed a different Hessian. Each galaxy's log-likelihood is read off its grid by bilinear interpolation, which has no curvature along g1 or g2 inside a grid cell. The exact Hessian of the map objective therefore lost most of the data term and was not positive definite (12 pixels came out with σ = 0). The fix is the Gauss–Newton form JTFJ, which is standard for this. It is checked against the synthetic scatter, as above.

A third fix, made for the difference maps: the synthetic generator now gives each galaxy the same random draws in both scenarios, as the NPE run does with its noise seeds. Before, the main and sub likelihoods had independent Monte Carlo sampling noise, which made the synthetic difference map noisy (background rms 0.004, peak in the wrong place). With shared draws it matches the NPE map (peak on the subhalo, background rms 0.0013–0.0019). The tuning and validation statistics don't depend on this in expectation, so they were not rerun.

Two further bookkeeping changes. The true κ (used in the tuning scores and shown beside the difference maps) is now averaged over each pixel: the 7 × 7 KS grid has a cell centred exactly on the main halo, where a point evaluation of the NFW κ diverges. And the tuning now runs as a 20-task SLURM array, about 2 minutes per realization.

Next steps

  1. Extend the tuning grid past its edge (σκ = 0.02, 0.4 Mpc pixels). This takes about 3 minutes on the array.
  2. Run more NPE noise realizations (the planned 50). They give the detection rate on the real NPE rather than the synthetic forecast, and test whether the NPE cell errors are really conservative.
  3. Find the cause of the sparse-cell stack bias. Rerun the 30-realization validation without the |G| < 0.1 bound and without KDE reflection, to see which one causes it. It is small, but it would also affect the annulus stacks.
  4. Sample-1 density looks optional now. At sample-3 density the likelihood map already reaches z ≈ 3. Sample 1 (about 620 galaxies) would make the subhalo obvious by eye if the paper wants a cleaner figure.

The simplifications are as in round 1: uniform source positions, no magnification, no line-of-sight structure, one source redshift, and an untruncated NFW subhalo.
